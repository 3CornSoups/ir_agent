You inventory the attached reference assets for a Ref2VA / full-reference job (official six-section rewrite comes later).

Assets are numbered in upload order:
- images → <Picture 1>, <Picture 2>, ...
- videos → <Video 1>, <Video 2>, ...
- audios → <Audio N>, ...

For each asset write what it actually contains and what it can reasonably provide:
identity / costume / style / lighting / motion / pacing / soundtrack / first-or-last-frame / storyboard.

Distinguish:
- appearance or style sheet (usually becomes a <Subject>, not a standalone Picture line later)
- concrete first / last / key frame
- storyboard / 4-grid / 9-grid sheet (one file, many cells)
- source video to edit or continue
- audio to copy vs only to reference

Official rule: one reference asset may provide multiple subjects. Cover a Picture by listing every subject that appears in it.
For people or animals, record clothing items, colors, and body coverage (covered vs exposed). Never omit visible garments.

Composite / grid stills (2x2, 3x3, 4-panel, 9-panel, comic strip, storyboard sheet):
Vision models often lock onto one cell. Prevent that.
For each <Picture N>:
1) First line: Layout: single scene | 2x2 grid | 3x3 grid | 1xN strip | other.
2) If it is a grid/strip, scan left-to-right, top-to-bottom. One note per cell:
   cell r,c — who/what, clothing, pose, setting.
3) Then "Subjects in this picture:": every distinct person, animal, object, costume, scene, or pose identity.
   Same identity across cells = one Subject with per-cell pose/angle/costume deltas.
   Different identities = separate Subjects, each citing this <Picture N> and the cells.
4) Completeness: a 2x2 needs 4 cell notes; a 3x3 needs 9. Never let the most salient panel stand in for the whole sheet.

Do not invent assets that were not attached.
Do not mention aspect ratio, resolution, fps, or canvas size.
Do not output MiniMax six-section fields yet. Plain labeled notes only.

After the notes for each asset, add a short paragraph starting with "What it can provide:" and list the reusable dimensions the asset can feed into subject_definitions, for example: character identity / costume & style / lighting / motion & action / camera pacing & cuts / soundtrack or voice timbre / first-or-last-frame anchor / storyboard structure. Distinguish "an asset that defines a reusable subject" from "a concrete frame anchor" and "a source video to edit or continue".
